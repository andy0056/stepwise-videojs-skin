Stepwise

The player that waits for you. For busy hands.

Three things to try

  1. Press play and let a step finish. The video stops on the step's last frame. Nothing moves until you choose Next step or Repeat.
  2. Say “next”. Turn on Say “next” on the plate, then keep your hands where they are and say it. “Repeat”, “slower”, “back” and “step three” work too, and “what can I say” lists the rest. The microphone opens only while the player is waiting.
  3. Use a knuckle. While it waits, a tap anywhere on the picture goes on to the next step, and the screen stays awake so a phone on the counter does not lock itself. Leave it a minute and it keeps you company.

Why

Most video is watched. Some of it is followed: a recipe, a repair, a knot, a stretch. Following means the viewer is doing something else with their hands and their eyes, and every player makes them stop that to chase a thin bar backwards by a few seconds.

Stepwise moves the pause to where it is wanted. A video's chapters are its steps. Each step is one segment of the seek bar with its number on it, and when a step ends the player holds its last frame, the finished result, for as long as the viewer needs.

It is still a player for someone else's site. Play, seek, time, volume, captions and full screen are where people expect them. A video with no chapters gets none of the step controls and plays as usual.

Details

  • Readable over any frame. Controls sit on a dark plate whose colour carries the transparency; text and icons stay opaque. The idle hairline is dark-edged, so it holds over white footage too.
  • State by shape, not colour alone. The switch moves its knob, the current segment thickens, and the step tag changes its number.
  • Voice, with limits. The button appears only in browsers that can recognise speech, and it is off until you turn it on. The microphone is closed whenever the video is playing, so the video cannot trigger itself. Where the browser has an on-device model the audio stays on your device; otherwise the browser's own speech service does the recognising. Stepwise never receives or stores audio.
  • Busy hands. During a wait the whole picture is the Next button. The screen is kept awake for up to half an hour of waiting, where the browser allows it, and released the moment the video plays on.
  • Repeat, slower. One button replays the step at half speed, then gives you your own speed back. S does the same.
  • Keyboard. N next, B back, R repeat, W wait on or off, and the space bar carries on. Every control is reachable, focus is always outlined, and when the wait plate appears focus lands on Next step if you were already in the player.
  • Calm on request. Reduced motion removes the slides and scales. Reduced transparency and more contrast make every plate solid. Forced colours use the system palette.
  • Small players. Under 480 px the numbers leave the segments, Back a step folds into Repeat, and the plate's buttons take the full width.
  • Public knobs. --media-accent-color, --media-accent-text-color, --media-border-radius and --media-font-family work as they do on the packaged skins.
